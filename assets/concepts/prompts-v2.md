# Concept prompts — v2 (gothic stop-motion)

Direction (Developer decision, 2026-10-19): inspired by the look of classic gothic
stop-motion holiday films, not copied. Prompts never name a film, studio, or character.

Use in Grok Imagine, 9:16, generate many variations. Paste the **base prompt** alone first;
then try the **variation lines** appended to it to push composition.

## Base prompt

Vertical 9:16 illustration of a handcrafted stop-motion miniature set at night, photographed
like a film still. Sculpted clay, carved foam, and painted wood textures with visible
handmade detail. Palette: near-black, cold grey, deep teal, muted violet, with warm orange
and sickly yellow-green glows. Three zones stacked top to bottom, clearly separated by depth:

TOP — a huge pale full moon behind a tall, thin hill whose peak curls over into a loose
spiral; a few bare twisted trees and a crooked iron fence on its slope.

MIDDLE — a crooked gothic town on a ridge: tall narrow leaning houses with bent spires,
pointed roofs, uneven chimneys, a central tower with a small clock, glowing orange windows,
jack-o'-lanterns on steps, a stone archway gate.

BOTTOM — a dark forest clearing of grey, gnarled, leafless trees in a ring. One wide tree
trunk in the center has a small arched door carved into it, shaped and painted like a
decorated Christmas tree with a star and ornaments, glowing warmly around its edges.
A winding pale path leads from the bottom of the frame to that door.

Spooky but playful, suitable for children. Clear air with no fog or mist, no characters,
no text, no lettering, no logos. Distinct foreground, middle, and background planes with
clean silhouettes and minimal overlap so the image can be cut into layers.

## Variation lines (append one)

1. Low camera angle; the town ridge sits higher and the forest clearing fills the lower half.
2. The moon is off-center to the left; the spiral hill curls to the right.
3. More vertical town: very tall, thin, wobbly buildings with striped awnings.
4. The forest trees frame the bottom like an arch; the door tree is larger and closer.
5. Colder palette: moonlight blue-grey with a single warm glow from the door.
6. Warmer palette: more orange window light and lanterns along the path.

## Why these choices

- **No fog/mist:** the professor added fog in code between layers. Baked-in fog
  blurs edges and makes layer cutting harder.
- **No characters:** keeps every layer static and original; characters can be added later.
- **One door only:** the forest has only the Christmas-tree door (Developer decision).
- **"Minimal overlap":** high-detail scenes cut less cleanly than flat papercraft did;
  this asks the generator to keep depth planes separable.
